# Local open data beats free geocoders here

The best zero-cost way to improve the pins is to stop relying on rate-limited geocoders for the fallback tiers. Instead, build a small offline gazetteer from three open sources and refresh it now and then. The first is PSGC-keyed barangay polygons (faeldon, 3Q 2026). The second is named residential features from a daily Geofabrik OpenStreetMap extract. The third is Overture Places, which is mostly Meta/Facebook pages and was the only source in testing that held branded Pag-IBIG-type developments OSM tends to miss. Add a few developer-published coordinates (Lumina) and a moderated "wrong pin" override file. This matters because the current tiers drop from **0.2 km median error (subdivision) to 0.85 km (barangay) to 3.8 km (town)**. The biggest gain therefore comes from moving listings up into the subdivision tier, not from shaving metres off any one tier. No free proprietary geocoder (HERE, TomTom, Esri, Mapbox, Radar, Azure) allows both permanent storage in a JSON file and display on Leaflet/OSM without a card. That leaves OSM-based services and open datasets as the only compliant toolset. Every source recommended below allows permanent storage with attribution. The one licensing wrinkle is ODbL share-alike once OSM-derived coordinates are published in the same database as everything else. Nobody has measured accuracy gains for the new tiers. The 1/3 of listings with real Pag-IBIG GPS give you a free holdout set to measure each tier before you trust it.

## Proprietary geocoders fail the storage or map test

The constraints are free, no card, results stored permanently in a daily-rebuilt JSON, and display on Leaflet/OSM. Every commercial-data provider checked fails at least one of them, and each failure is written into its terms. **Mapbox** says "You may only use responses from the Geocoding API in conjunction with a Mapbox map". Permanent results that may be cached "requires that you have a valid credit card on file" ([Mapbox Geocoding docs](https://docs.mapbox.com/api/search/geocoding/)). **Esri** gives 20K free geocodes a month only when they are *not stored*. Stored geocodes ("Store or persist geocoding service results", `forStorage=true`) have no free tier and cost $4 per 1,000 ([ArcGIS pricing](https://location.arcgis.com/pricing/)). **HERE** bars keeping geocoding results for more than 30 days without a paid Permanent Geocoding licence ([HERE KB](https://docs.here.com/here-kb/docs/permanent-geocoding-overview-licensing-and-usage-rules)). Its KB also says "You provide a payment method up front" ([HERE billing KB](https://docs.here.com/here-kb/docs/how-is-billing-for-a-developer-here-platform-account)). **Radar** states "Radar does not currently offer a free tier" ([Radar pricing](https://radar.com/pricing)). **TomTom** now lists its Search API as "Free 2.5K monthly" with "no credit card needed" ([TomTom pricing](https://docs.tomtom.com/pricing)). That is a monthly figure, where TomTom was long known for a daily one. TomTom also prohibits building derivative databases from results, according to a competitor's summary of its terms ([Geocodio comparison](https://www.geocod.io/geocoding-terms-of-use-comparison)).

The OSM-based services remain, and they only know what OSM contributors have mapped. **Nominatim (OSMF)** requires "an absolute maximum of 1 request per second" and a real User-Agent, says results "must be cached", and limits recurring scripts to 4 requests per minute ([OSMF Nominatim policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/)). **OpenCage** says "You can store the data returned by the API permanently" and requires no card, but its free tier is a "testing only" trial ([OpenCage pricing](https://opencagedata.com/pricing)). **Geoapify** allows free commercial use "with some limitations" and requires OSM plus Geoapify attribution. Its T&C page says nothing about storage ([Geoapify T&C](https://www.geoapify.com/terms-and-conditions/)). **LocationIQ** gives 5,000 requests a day but caps caching at "upto 48 hours" ([LocationIQ pricing](https://locationiq.com/pricing)). A JSON rebuilt daily from fresh calls arguably fits that window, but the cache cap makes LocationIQ a poor permanent store. **Stadia** and **MapTiler** free plans are non-commercial ([Stadia pricing](https://stadiamaps.com/pricing), [MapTiler pricing](https://www.maptiler.com/cloud/pricing/)).

| Provider | Free, no card | Permanent storage | Leaflet/OSM display | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominatim (OSMF) | Yes | Yes (caching required) | Yes, OSM attribution | Keep, but only for new or changed listings |
| OpenCage | Yes (2,500/day trial) | Yes, explicitly | Yes | Second opinion, "testing only" |
| Geoapify | Yes | Not addressed | Yes, attribution | Usable, but storage is unconfirmed |
| LocationIQ | Yes (5,000/day) | 48-hour cache cap | Yes, backlink | Weak fit |
| HERE / Esri / Mapbox / TomTom / Radar | Fails on card, storage, map or tier | — | — | Exclude |

Since all the compliant services draw on the same OSM data, querying several of them adds little. The better move is to download OSM and search it locally. That removes the rate-limit ceiling and allows fuzzy matching that Nominatim's free-text search cannot do.

## Overture Places finds the branded developments OSM misses

Overture Places is the strongest new source for the subdivision tier. It is free, needs no account, and stores and displays without restriction under CDLA-Permissive-2.0, with Apache-2.0 for Foursquare rows and CC0 for AllThePlaces rows ([Overture attribution](https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/)). A live DuckDB query against release `2026-11-18.0` on public S3 pulled **1,246,673 Philippine places in about 50 seconds into an 86.5 MB Parquet file**. Of these, **97% came from Meta** (1,213,248 rows), with Foursquare 17,959 and Microsoft 6,393 ([method per Overture DuckDB guide](https://docs.overturemaps.org/getting-data/duckdb/)). The query takes only `INSTALL httpfs; LOAD httpfs; SET s3_region='us-west-2';` plus a `read_parquet('s3://overturemaps-us-west-2/release/2026-11-18.0/theme=places/type=place/*', hive_partitioning=1)` filtered to bbox lon 116.9–126.7 and lat 4.5–21.2 with `addresses[1].country='PH'`. All five test developments had records. Lumina Pandi appeared as a Microsoft-sourced `home_developer` row at confidence 0.85 (14.89707, 120.98417). Deca Homes South Bacolod had about eight records clustered within roughly 0.5 km. Symfoni Nichols records clustered in Guadalupe, Cebu.

The data is noisy in ways that rule out naive matching. Only **71 PH rows are categorized `condominium`**, and no "subdivision" category exists. Developments show up as `real_estate_service`, `home_developer`, or even `historic_site` and `homeless_shelter`. Many rows are sales-agent Facebook pages pinned at the agent's home. The roughly ten "Urban Deca Homes Ortigas" pages were scattered as far as Imus, Cavite. The two best candidates disagreed by about 5 km. The workable recipe has five steps. Ignore category. Drop names matching agent or rental patterns (`by |agent|for rent|for sale|pad|staycation|unit \d`). Normalize names. Require the point to fall inside the listing's PSGC city polygon, or within about 3 km of the barangay. When several candidates survive, take the medoid of the cluster and treat a wide spread as ambiguous. Store the GERS `id` with the coordinate, because it stays stable across monthly releases. Overture's guide warns that "joining this data with OSM may require the ODbL license for the resulting derivative database" ([Places guide](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/places/)). That affects how the published JSON is licensed, covered in the conclusion. Required attribution is "Overture Maps Foundation" plus a "Data from Meta" line for each source.

The other two POI datasets add little. **Foursquare OS Places** (Apache-2.0, with category 12122 "Apartment or Condo") now sits behind a free gated Hugging Face account or a Places Portal token instead of the old anonymous bucket ([HF dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/foursquare/fsq-os-places), [FSQ access docs](https://docs.foursquare.com/data-products/docs/access-fsq-os-places)). Overture already carries about 18k Foursquare PH rows, so the standalone download only adds recall for condos, and that was not tested. **Wikidata** is CC0, but a SPARQL sweep for PH residential labels returned only 116 items: famous Makati and BGC towers and old villages such as Dasmariñas Village and BF Homes. It had none of Lumina, Deca, Camella or SMDC ([Wikidata Query Service](https://query.wikidata.org/sparql?format=json&query=SELECT%20%3Fitem%20%3FitemLabel%20%3Fcoord%20WHERE%20%7B%20%3Fitem%20wdt%3AP17%20wd%3AQ928%3B%20wdt%3AP625%20%3Fcoord%3B%20rdfs%3Alabel%20%3FitemLabel.%20FILTER(LANG(%3FitemLabel)%3D%22en%22)%20FILTER(REGEX(%3FitemLabel%2C%20%22(Residences%7CSubdivision%7CVillage%7CHomes%7CTower%7CCondominium%7CVillas)%24%22))%20%7D%20LIMIT%20300)). "Symfoni Nichols" returns nothing ([Wikidata search](https://www.wikidata.org/w/index.php?search=Symfoni+Nichols&title=Special:Search&ns0=1)).

Developer websites are a narrow but precise source. **Lumina Homes** embeds exact project coordinates in each of its 33 project pages, for example `new google.maps.LatLng(14.899562, 120.983266)` for Lumina Pandi ([Lumina Pandi](https://www.lumina.com.ph/property-listing/property/lumina-pandi)). That pin sits roughly 0.3 km from Overture's Microsoft-sourced Lumina Pandi row, which is good mutual confirmation. **8990 Holdings** (Deca/Urban Deca) lists about 24 projects with a "Brgy. X, City" address and DHSUD LTS number but no coordinates ([8990 project sitemap](https://8990holdings.com/project-sitemap.xml), [Deca Homes Cabanatuan](https://8990holdings.com/projects/deca-homes-cabanatuan/)). **Camella** exposes about 113 projects with province and city through an open WordPress REST API ([Camella REST](https://www.camella.com.ph/wp-json/wp/v2/properties?per_page=2)). **PHirst** gives street and barangay addresses ([PHirst Naic](https://www.phirstparkhomes.com/projects/naic/)). Fiesta Communities disallows all crawling ([Fiesta robots.txt](https://www.fiestacommunities.ph/robots.txt)). Lumina's terms prohibit "reproduction" of material described as "design, layout, look, appearance and graphics" ([Lumina T&C](https://www.lumina.com.ph/terms-and-conditions/)). A hand-maintained table of about 30 coordinate pairs is a far weaker target than mirrored content, but it is still a grey area. Treat these as a one-time, politely crawled (10 s delay) seed list and QA cross-check, not a scraper in the daily job. Developer catalogues cover only currently selling projects, and the older phases that dominate foreclosure lists will often be missing.

## PSGC barangay polygons should replace the Nominatim barangay search

The barangay tier currently depends on Nominatim resolving a barangay name. That can fail outright or match a same-named barangay in another town: "Poblacion I" alone has **45 exact matches nationwide** ([faeldon CSV](https://github.com/faeldon/philippines-psgc-shapefiles/releases/download/v1.0.1/PH_Adm4_BgySubMuns.csv)). The fix is deterministic. **faeldon/philippines-json-maps** (release v1.1.1, PSGC 3Q 2026, published 2026-11-07) provides barangay polygons split per city or municipality in GeoJSON and TopoJSON. The whole-country low-resolution TopoJSON zip is **3.8 MB** and the medium one 5.8 MB ([GitHub releases](https://api.github.com/repos/faeldon/philippines-json-maps/releases)). The companion shapefile release covers **42,010 barangays, 41,865 of them with the original COD-AB shape**. It is in EPSG:32651 and needs reprojecting to 4326 ([faeldon shapefiles](https://github.com/faeldon/philippines-psgc-shapefiles)). The scripts are MIT-licensed, but the boundaries are **CC BY-IGO**, so the map must credit NAMRIA and PSA via OCHA COD-AB ([faeldon json-maps](https://github.com/faeldon/philippines-json-maps)). geoBoundaries stops at ADM3 for the Philippines ([geoBoundaries PHL](https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/gbOpen/PHL/ALL/)). The older altcoder repo is archived at 4Q 2023 ([altcoder](https://github.com/altcoder/philippines-psgc-shapefiles)).

Here is the implementation. Precompute one **point-on-surface** (turf `pointOnFeature` or DuckDB `ST_PointOnSurface`) per barangay into a PSGC-keyed table of about 42k rows, roughly 2–3 MB. A geometric centroid can fall outside concave or multipart barangays, while a point-on-surface cannot. Match listing text to PSGC codes, never raw names, and always scope to the already-resolved city's `adm3_psgc`. Normalize both sides: uppercase, ñ→n, expand Sto./Sta./Pob., unify Roman numerals ("II-A" vs "2-A"), and treat parenthetical text as aliases. "PEREZ (LUCBANAN)" has no exact PSGC row, but plain "Perez" does. OSM admin relations carry the 10-digit PSGC in `ref`; Parañaque's r122940 has `ref=1381000000` ([Overpass result](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). That gives a clean join key between OSM and PSGC.

The polygons also earn a second role as a **validator for every other tier**. Each candidate from Pag-IBIG GPS, subdivision medians, Overture, OSM or Nominatim must fall inside the stated city, and ideally inside the stated barangay with a 100–200 m buffer for low-resolution edges. A candidate that fails the city test is rejected. One that passes the city test but fails the barangay test is kept and flagged. Overture's scattered agent pages and Nominatim's same-name mismatches are the main sources of large errors, and this check catches both. A point-on-surface will not by itself beat the 0.85 km median of successful Nominatim barangay hits, because both are "somewhere in the barangay". The gain comes from coverage, determinism and the elimination of wrong-town outliers. A finer refinement is untested. Within the barangay polygon, place the pin at the density centre of small residential building footprints rather than the geometric middle. Google Open Buildings v3 (CC BY 4.0 or ODbL, with `area_in_meters` and `confidence`) and Microsoft's footprints (CDLA-Permissive 2.0, with per-building `height`) both cover the Philippines ([Google Open Buildings](https://sites.research.google/gr/open-buildings/), [Microsoft footprints](https://github.com/microsoft/GlobalMLBuildingFootprints)). Settlements cluster in part of a barangay while farmland or water fills the rest, which is common outside NCR, so the density centre should land closer to real homes. Extract footprints once per needed city, not daily. The PH tiles run to several GB.

## A local OSM extract retires the Nominatim subdivision bottleneck

Nominatim's free-text search wants exact names. The public Overpass server was overloaded during testing, returning "502 … server is currently at capacity" on area queries, and its policy discourages app-backend use ([OSM Wiki: Overpass API](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API), [Overpass commons doc](https://dev.overpass-api.de/overpass-doc/en/preface/commons.html)). The rate-limit-free alternative is Geofabrik's **`philippines-latest.osm.pbf`: 586 MB, ODbL, updated daily** ([Geofabrik Philippines](https://download.geofabrik.de/asia/philippines.html)). Run `osmium tags-filter` once a week or once a month for `landuse=residential`, `residential=*`, `place=neighbourhood|suburb|quarter|village`, `building=apartments|residential` and admin boundaries. Export the named features with a point-on-surface. Tag each with its PSGC city and barangay by point-in-polygon. The result is a table of a few MB that Node or DuckDB can fuzzy-match with no request limits. These commands were not run during the research, and the size of the filtered subset is unmeasured.

Match names the same way for OSM and Overture. Strip generic tokens (SUBDIVISION, SUBD, VILLAGE, HOMES, RESIDENCES, TOWER n, PHASE n, BLK/LOT) and keep developer prefixes (CAMELLA, LUMINA, DECA, SMDC) as tie-breakers. Compare with token-set or Jaro-Winkler similarity at about 0.85, and require city containment. An OSM `landuse=residential` polygon's point-on-surface is a better subdivision pin than a Facebook page's pin. Prefer OSM when both match, and use Overture to fill OSM's gaps. Keep Nominatim only for new or changed listings that miss both local indexes. At 4 requests per minute that is about 100 lookups in a 25-minute job ([OSMF policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/nominatim/)), which is plenty for a daily delta.

The resulting priority stack, with validation applied at every step, looks like this:

| Rank | Source | Expected precision | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Approved crowdsourced or manual override | Exact | New |
| 2 | Pag-IBIG's own GPS (validated in city polygon) | Exact | Existing, about 1/3 of listings |
| 3 | Median of other Pag-IBIG GPS in same subdivision | 0.2 km measured | Existing |
| 4 | Developer-published project coordinate (Lumina seed table) | Project gate or centre | New, narrow coverage |
| 5 | Local OSM named residential feature, point-on-surface | Subdivision-level, unmeasured | New, replaces most Nominatim calls |
| 6 | Overture Places match (filtered, medoid, in-polygon) | Subdivision-level when clustered, unmeasured | New |
| 7 | Nominatim subdivision search (delta only) | 0.2 km measured | Existing, demoted |
| 8 | PSGC barangay point-on-surface (optionally footprint-weighted) | About 0.85 km, no wrong-town errors | Replaces Nominatim barangay search |
| 9 | City/municipality point-on-surface | 3.8 km measured | Existing |

Here is how to put a number on "expected accuracy gain" without inventing one. Run every tier blind on the listings that have real GPS, then record each tier's hit rate and median and 90th-percentile error. Every listing that moves from tier 8 or 9 into a subdivision-level tier cuts its typical error from 0.85–3.8 km to the order of 0.2–0.5 km. So the overall gain equals the share of non-GPS listings that tiers 4–6 newly resolve. That share has not been measured. The spot tests suggest branded developments (Lumina, Deca, Camella) resolve well, while small unbranded subdivisions and condos with only agent pages will not.

## Uncertainty display and moderated corrections close the remaining gap

Some listings will never get better than a barangay or a town, and the honest response is to show that rather than a confident pin. Airbnb's precedent is "a small circle surrounded by a shaded circle", accurate to within about half a mile ([Airbnb Help](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2141/how-will-my-listings-location-be-shown-on-the-map)). The measured medians give a defensible scale. Store a `precision` field per listing. Show exact-tier pins as solid markers. Give subdivision-tier pins a hollow marker with an `L.circle` of about 200 m, the measured median. Draw barangay-tier listings as the barangay polygon or a dashed circle of about 0.85–1 km, with no pin. Town-tier listings get a city-scale circle labelled as approximate. A distinct cluster colour keeps low-precision listings from looking exact. Put a "Report wrong location" link beside the precision label in each popup.

Report handling can stay zero-cost and backend-free with **GitHub Issue Forms**. A prefilled `issues/new?template=wrong-pin.yml&title=...` link opens a form, and the template's `labels:` key auto-tags it. A prefilled `labels=` URL parameter would fail for reporters who lack label permission ([GitHub Docs: creating an issue](https://docs.github.com/en/issues/tracking-your-work-with-issues/using-issues/creating-an-issue), [issue form syntax](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/using-templates-to-encourage-useful-issues-and-pull-requests/syntax-for-issue-forms)). A workflow on `issues: types: [labeled]` parses approved reports into `overrides.json`, and the daily build applies it first ([workflow triggers](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/writing-workflows/choosing-when-your-workflow-runs/events-that-trigger-workflows)). Most Filipino home buyers have no GitHub account, so add a Google Form as an anonymous second channel, with responses published as CSV and approved by hand. Formspree's 50 submissions a month and Supabase's pause after a week of inactivity make both poor fits ([Formspree limits](https://help.formspree.io/articles/account-management/account-limits), [Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)). For a public repo the daily job runs free on standard runners ([Actions billing](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/managing-billing-for-your-products/managing-billing-for-github-actions/about-billing-for-github-actions)). Use an off-the-hour cron, because top-of-hour runs can be delayed or dropped and schedules are disabled after 60 days without repository activity ([workflow triggers](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/writing-workflows/choosing-when-your-workflow-runs/events-that-trigger-workflows)).

Each verified correction is also a chance to fix OSM itself. Following WikiProject Philippines, tag subdivisions `landuse=residential` + `residential=single_family` and condo complexes `residential=apartments`, with `operator=*` for the developer ([OSM PH conventions](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Philippines/Mapping_conventions)). Trace only from Esri World Imagery, which is explicitly permitted ([OSM wiki: Esri](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Esri)), or from Mapillary, whose CC BY-SA images may be used to derive OSM data ([Mapillary OSM](https://mapillary.com/osm)). Never trace from Google or Wikimapia, which OSM refuses to accept ([OSM wiki: Wikimapia](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Wikimapia)). The next extract refresh then resolves the subdivision for every future listing there. Two sources that sound useful are not. There is no open PH address-point dataset: OpenAddresses has no `ph` folder ([OpenAddresses](https://github.com/openaddresses/openaddresses)) and Overture's addresses theme omits the Philippines ([Overture addresses](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/addresses/)). So unit and lot numbers cannot be geocoded with open data. Subdivision-level placement is the realistic ceiling outside Pag-IBIG's own GPS and human corrections.

## Conclusion

The key shift is architectural. The accuracy ceiling comes from *which tier* a listing lands in, and the free geocoders all run on the same OSM data. Requery them and you get the same misses at a slower rate. Downloading Overture, Geofabrik and PSGC polygons once in a while turns geolocation into a local, testable matching problem with no rate limits. It also gives the existing tiers a polygon validator that removes the worst outliers, the wrong-town and agent-office pins, which hurt users more than a 0.85 km median. Whether tiers 4–6 lift 10% or 40% of non-GPS listings into the subdivision tier is the unknown that decides the payoff. The Pag-IBIG GPS holdout answers it in an afternoon, so measure before you rank Overture above or below OSM for good.

Licensing is the one place to decide on purpose. Overture, footprints, PSGC polygons and developer facts each carry light attribution duties. But mixing Nominatim/OSM-derived coordinates into a JSON that you publish makes the file, arguably, an ODbL derivative database, and Overture's own guide warns that OSM joins may pull in ODbL. That reading is unverified, not legal advice. The simplest stance is to publish the listing JSON under ODbL-compatible terms with a combined credit line ("© OpenStreetMap contributors; Overture Maps Foundation (Data from Meta, Microsoft, Foursquare); boundaries NAMRIA/PSA via OCHA COD-AB, CC BY-IGO"). Store a `source` and `precision` per pin so any one source's contribution can be traced or removed later.
